# APP 6 - Use or disclosure of personal information

APP 6 limits how an APP entity may use or disclose personal information it holds. The
general rule: only for the purpose for which it was collected (the primary purpose),
unless an exception applies.

## In plain English

Use information for the reason you collected it. To use it for a different reason, you
need either consent, a directly-related secondary purpose the individual would
reasonably expect, or one of the specific legal exceptions.

## Why it exists

APP 6 stops information collected for one purpose drifting into others without the
individual's knowledge.

## What APP 6 requires

- Personal information may only be used or disclosed for the primary purpose of
  collection, unless an exception applies.
- Exceptions include: consent; secondary use the individual would reasonably expect that
  is related to the primary purpose (or, for sensitive information, directly related);
  use or disclosure required or authorised by Australian law or a court order; permitted
  general situations (serious threat to life or safety, suspected unlawful activity,
  research where impracticable to seek consent and certain safeguards are in place);
  enforcement-related activities; and a specific permitted health situation.
- Note that disclosure to an overseas recipient additionally engages APP 8.

## Evidence

- Data flow diagrams showing every use and disclosure.
- Documented purpose for each.
- Consent records for sensitive information.
- Records of approvals for non-primary use (legal opinion, threat assessment).

## Where to look

- Marketing tooling integrations (e.g. sending support customer email addresses to
  marketing automation).
- Analytics and product-improvement pipelines (e.g. behavioural analytics from a service
  used for service-improvement and then for sale).
- Internal employee access to customer information (need-to-know vs. anyone-with-login).
- Mergers and acquisitions: data shared in due diligence.
- AI / ML training data: was the data collected with training as a purpose?

## Common pitfalls

- "Service improvement" treated as covering anything.
- Marketing list derived from a support ticketing system without notice or consent.
- Internal data warehouse with broad access that effectively constitutes disclosure
  inside the entity.
- Use of personal information to train a model without considering whether that was a
  reasonable expectation.
