# APP 13 - Correction of personal information

APP 13 requires APP entities to take reasonable steps to correct personal information they
hold, both on request from the individual and where the entity is satisfied the
information is inaccurate, out-of-date, incomplete, irrelevant or misleading.

## In plain English

If someone tells you their data is wrong, fix it. If you spot that data is wrong, fix
it. Tell anyone you disclosed the wrong data to that it was wrong, if asked.

## Why it exists

APP 13 closes the loop on data quality. Without the right to correct, individuals could
not protect themselves from harm caused by inaccurate records.

## What APP 13 requires

- Where the entity is satisfied that personal information it holds is inaccurate,
  out-of-date, incomplete, irrelevant or misleading, take reasonable steps to correct
  it.
- On request from an individual, take reasonable steps to correct personal information.
- If correction is refused, give written reasons and tell the individual how to complain.
- If the entity has previously disclosed the personal information to a third party and
  the individual requests, take reasonable steps to notify the third party of the
  correction, unless impracticable or unlawful.
- Associate a statement of correction with the personal information if the individual
  requests and the entity declines to correct.
- Response timeframes mirror APP 12 (agencies 30 days; organisations a reasonable period).
- No charge may be imposed.

## Evidence

- Documented process for receiving and handling correction requests.
- Workflow ticket history showing corrections made, refusals with reasons, notifications
  to downstream recipients.
- Audit log of changes to personal information showing who made the change and why.

## Where to look

- Customer support and complaints workflows.
- Account settings allowing self-service corrections.
- Downstream systems that received personal information from the system of record:
  marketing tooling, analytics, third-party processors.
- Audit log of personal-information changes.

## Common pitfalls

- Correction made in one system but not propagated to copies elsewhere.
- "We can't correct it because the law says we have to keep records." Records can usually
  be supplemented with corrections rather than deleted.
- Refusal without written reasons.
- No notification to third parties downstream.
- Self-service correction tooling that allows changes without audit trail.
