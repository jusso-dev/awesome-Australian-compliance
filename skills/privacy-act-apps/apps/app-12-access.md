# APP 12 - Access to personal information

APP 12 requires APP entities to give individuals access to their personal information on
request, subject to certain exceptions. Different rules apply to agencies (which must
also comply with the Freedom of Information Act 1982 for many requests) and organisations.

## In plain English

When someone asks for their data, give it to them. There are specific reasons you can
refuse, but they're narrow. Respond within the required timeframe.

## Why it exists

APP 12 gives individuals visibility over what an entity holds about them. It enables the
correction obligation in APP 13.

## What APP 12 requires

- On request from an individual, give the individual access to the personal information
  the entity holds about the individual, unless an exception applies.
- For agencies, decisions must comply with the Freedom of Information Act 1982 in many
  cases; APP 12 supplements rather than replaces FOI.
- For organisations, the exceptions include where access would pose a serious threat to
  life or safety, would have an unreasonable impact on others' privacy, the request is
  frivolous or vexatious, the information relates to existing or anticipated legal
  proceedings (subject to discovery rules), giving access would prejudice negotiations,
  would be unlawful, would prejudice enforcement-related activities, would reveal
  evaluative information generated in commercially sensitive decision-making, and others.
- Response timeframes apply (agencies: 30 days; organisations: a reasonable period,
  generally interpreted as 30 days for most purposes).
- Where access is refused, written reasons must be provided.
- Access must be given in the manner requested if reasonable and practicable.

## Evidence

- Documented process for receiving and handling access requests.
- Workflow ticket history for past requests.
- Response time metrics: median and 95th percentile time-to-response.
- Refusal records with documented basis for each.

## Where to look

- Customer support tooling.
- Privacy enquiry inbox.
- Identity verification step before disclosing (often a sticking point).
- Data export functionality in self-service portals.

## Common pitfalls

- No documented process: requests handled ad-hoc.
- Identity verification asking for more information than the request itself reveals.
- Response timeframes routinely missed.
- "We don't have that data" used incorrectly because the search did not cover all
  systems.
- Format-of-disclosure inflexibility ("we can only give you a PDF").
