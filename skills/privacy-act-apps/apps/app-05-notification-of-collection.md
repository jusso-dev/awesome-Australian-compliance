# APP 5 - Notification of the collection of personal information

APP 5 requires the entity to take reasonable steps to notify the individual of certain
matters at the time of, or as soon as practicable after, collection.

## In plain English

When you collect personal information, tell the person. Tell them who you are, why
you're collecting it, who you'll disclose it to, and how they can complain. Tell them
about overseas disclosure if relevant.

## Why it exists

APP 5 is the "moment of collection" disclosure. It complements the broader policy in
APP 1 with a just-in-time notice that informs the individual at the point that matters.

## What APP 5 requires

The entity must take reasonable steps to notify the individual of:

- The entity's identity and contact details.
- The fact and circumstances of collection (especially if collected indirectly).
- Whether the collection is required or authorised by law.
- The purposes of collection.
- The main consequences for the individual if the information is not collected.
- Any other APP entity, body or person to which the entity usually discloses personal
  information of that kind.
- Information about the APP entity's APP privacy policy.
- Whether the entity is likely to disclose the information to overseas recipients, and
  the countries where they are likely to be located (if practicable).

## Evidence

- Collection notices at every collection point.
- Notice content matched to the system's actual practice.
- Layered notice design where appropriate (short notice at collection, long notice
  available on request).

## Where to look

- Registration and account-creation forms.
- Contact-us forms.
- API documentation for third-party callers submitting personal information.
- Mobile app onboarding screens.
- Phone scripts for call centre intake.

## Common pitfalls

- Linking to the privacy policy and treating that as notice. The policy may not be
  specific to the collection point.
- Notice that omits overseas disclosure ("we may disclose your information to our
  service providers" without naming jurisdictions).
- Notice that uses legal language nobody reads, where a layered approach would be clearer.
- Notice that lists historical purposes but not current ones.
