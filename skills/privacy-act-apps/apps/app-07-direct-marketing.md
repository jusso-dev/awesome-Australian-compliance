# APP 7 - Direct marketing

APP 7 restricts the use of personal information for direct marketing. The general rule
for organisations is that direct marketing requires either the individual having
provided the information for that purpose with a reasonable expectation, or consent
where the information was provided for a different primary purpose. Sensitive
information requires consent.

APP 7 generally does not apply to Commonwealth agencies but does apply to private-
sector APP entities. Several specific carve-outs and interactions with the Spam Act 2003
and Do Not Call Register Act 2006 apply.

## In plain English

Don't use personal information to market unless you have a clear right to. Always offer a
simple, free way to opt out. Stop on request.

## Why it exists

Direct marketing is the highest-volume use of personal information and the most common
source of complaint to the OAIC. APP 7 plus the Spam Act and Do Not Call frameworks
together govern this.

## What APP 7 requires (private-sector)

- Direct marketing using personal information collected from the individual requires the
  individual to have a reasonable expectation of marketing and a simple way to opt out.
- Direct marketing using personal information collected from someone other than the
  individual requires consent or impracticality of obtaining it, plus a simple opt-out.
- Sensitive information requires consent.
- Each marketing communication must include a prominent statement that the individual
  may opt out, or have an easy way to do so.
- An entity must honour an opt-out request within a reasonable period and at no cost.
- An individual may request the source of the information used for marketing.

## Evidence

- Consent records where consent is the basis.
- Marketing list lineage: where did each subscriber come from, and what basis applies.
- Opt-out handling: how requests are received, how soon they're honoured.
- Audit trail for unsubscribe processing.

## Where to look

- Marketing automation platforms (Salesforce Marketing Cloud, HubSpot, Braze, Mailchimp,
  agency equivalents).
- Customer data platforms that join CRM data to marketing tooling.
- Web form integrations creating subscribers.
- Acquired marketing lists from third parties.

## Common pitfalls

- "Pre-ticked" consent boxes (not valid consent).
- Marketing implied consent from a single-purpose collection (a support ticket).
- Opt-out link in tiny font or requiring a login.
- Continuing to send marketing for weeks after opt-out due to batch processes.
- Marketing list from a third party with no documented basis.

## Cross-references

- APP 6 (use): the general use rule that APP 7 narrows for marketing.
