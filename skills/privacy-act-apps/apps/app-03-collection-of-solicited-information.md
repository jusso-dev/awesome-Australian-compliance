# APP 3 - Collection of solicited personal information

APP 3 governs what personal information an APP entity may collect when it has asked for
it. The headline rule: only collect what is reasonably necessary for, or directly related
to, one or more of the entity's functions or activities.

## In plain English

Collect only what you actually need. For sensitive information, get consent unless an
exception applies. Collect by lawful and fair means.

## Why it exists

APP 3 is the data-minimisation principle. It is upstream of every other privacy risk: if
the data is not collected, it cannot be misused or breached.

## What APP 3 requires

- For agencies: only collect personal information that is reasonably necessary for, or
  directly related to, one or more of the agency's functions or activities.
- For organisations: only collect personal information that is reasonably necessary for
  one or more of the organisation's functions or activities.
- Sensitive information may only be collected with consent (with exceptions for
  legally-required, serious-threat, taking-appropriate-action, enforcement-related,
  diplomatic and certain research purposes).
- Personal information must be collected by lawful and fair means.
- Personal information must be collected from the individual unless impracticable.

## Evidence

- Data inventory mapping each collected field to a function or activity.
- Documented decision when a field is collected: necessary or just convenient.
- Sensitive information collection points with corresponding consent records.
- Collection mechanism documentation (the form, the API, the integration).

## Where to look

- User registration and account-creation forms.
- Survey and feedback tools.
- KYC / identity verification flows.
- Integrations that pull personal information from third parties.
- Cookie banners that result in additional personal information collection.

## Common pitfalls

- "Just in case" fields. A registration form collecting date of birth, gender and
  postcode for a use case that does not require any of them.
- Sensitive information collected without explicit consent (health information collected
  by a non-health entity for marketing purposes).
- Indirect collection where direct would be practicable (buying personal information from
  data brokers).
- Information collected covertly (tracking pixels) without disclosure.
- Free-text fields where users routinely paste personal information not asked for, with
  no plan for what to do with it.
