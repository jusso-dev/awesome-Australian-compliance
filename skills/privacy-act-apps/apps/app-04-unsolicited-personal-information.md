# APP 4 - Dealing with unsolicited personal information

APP 4 covers personal information an APP entity receives that it did not ask for. The
entity must work out whether it could have collected the information under APP 3; if not,
it must destroy or de-identify the information unless it cannot lawfully do so.

## In plain English

If someone sends you personal information you didn't ask for, decide whether you could
have asked for it under APP 3. If you could, treat it as if you had. If you couldn't,
destroy or de-identify it (unless that's not lawful).

## Why it exists

APP 4 prevents passive accumulation of personal information. Without it, an entity could
collect-by-acquisition information it could not collect-by-request.

## What APP 4 requires

- On receipt of unsolicited personal information, determine within a reasonable time
  whether it could have been collected under APP 3.
- If yes, APPs 5 onward apply as if the information had been solicited.
- If no, destroy or de-identify the information as soon as practicable, provided it is
  lawful and reasonable to do so.

## Evidence

- Procedure for handling unsolicited submissions.
- Records of destruction or de-identification decisions.

## Where to look

- Customer support inboxes that receive unsolicited resumes, complaints with attached
  personal information, freedom-of-information requests with collateral information.
- "Contact us" forms where users paste arbitrary content.
- Recruitment systems handling unsolicited CVs.
- Crash logs and error reports that may include personal information not designed to be
  collected.

## Common pitfalls

- Treating unsolicited submissions as a free source of leads (storing CVs in a
  recruitment database when there was no advertised vacancy).
- Crash logs and analytics inadvertently capturing personal information with no
  scheduled destruction.
- No process: support inboxes accumulating sensitive content indefinitely.

## Cross-references

- APP 3 (collection): the test that determines whether APP 4 applies.
- APP 11.2 (destruction or de-identification): the related obligation when information
  is no longer needed.
