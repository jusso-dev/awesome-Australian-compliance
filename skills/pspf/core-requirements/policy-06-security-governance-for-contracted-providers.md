# PSPF Policy 6 - Security governance for contracted goods and service providers

This policy sets the security governance expectations when an entity contracts goods
or services that engage the entity's protective security.

Note: numbering and content may have shifted in the PSPF 2024 redesign.

## In plain English

When you contract a provider that handles your information, premises or services,
treat their security posture as part of yours. Don't outsource accountability.

## Key requirements

- Identify the protective security implications of each contract.
- Build security requirements into the procurement (security clauses, classification
  handling, audit rights, incident notification).
- Conduct due diligence on the provider's security posture before signing.
- Monitor the provider's compliance over the contract.
- Manage the security risks of sub-contracted parties.

## Evidence

- Security clauses in master services agreements and statements of work.
- Provider security assessments (IRAP letters, SOC 2 reports, custom assessments).
- Sub-contractor inventory and assurance.
- Records of incident notifications received from providers.
- Periodic reassessment.

## Where to look

- Procurement records.
- Contract management system.
- Vendor risk management platform.

## Common pitfalls

- Security clauses generic and not negotiated meaningfully.
- Provider's IRAP letter accepted without reading the SoA and the assessor's findings.
- Sub-contracting visibility absent.
- No process when a provider's posture changes mid-contract.
