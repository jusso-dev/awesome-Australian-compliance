# PSPF Policy 1 - Role of the accountable authority

This policy assigns ultimate responsibility for protective security to the accountable
authority of the entity (typically the agency head or board).

Note: PSPF policy numbering and content shifted in the 2024 redesign. Confirm against
the live PSPF release before quoting numbers. See [`../versions.md`](../versions.md).

## In plain English

The agency head is accountable for protective security. They cannot delegate the
accountability, only the operation.

## Why it exists

Without a single accountable individual, protective security drifts. Policy 1 ties the
buck-stops-here to the top of the entity.

## Key requirements

- The accountable authority is responsible for the security of the entity's people,
  information and assets.
- They must determine the entity's security risk tolerance, approve the security plan,
  and accept residual risk where appropriate.
- They appoint and empower a Chief Security Officer (CSO).
- They report on protective security performance to the relevant Minister and AGD.

## Evidence

- Letter of appointment of the CSO.
- Approved agency security plan.
- Records of residual risk acceptance by the accountable authority.
- Annual PSPF report to AGD signed at the accountable-authority level.

## Where to look

- Agency board minutes covering security matters.
- The agency's security governance charter.
- Delegations register.

## Common pitfalls

- Accountable authority disengaged from security; CSO operates without their cover.
- Risk acceptance decisions made by the CSO without authority delegation in writing.
