# Guidelines for Physical Security

This guideline covers physical protection of ICT facilities, equipment and removable
media. It interlocks with PSPF Policy 15 (physical security).

## In plain English

The room your servers are in, the cabinet they live in, the building entry points and
the people who can swipe in are part of the security perimeter. Document the zones and
who can enter them.

## Why it exists

A logically-hardened system in an unsecured rack is not a hardened system. Physical
controls cover the residual paths that bypass network and identity controls entirely.

## Key topics

- Facility certification level (commercial-grade, government Zone-rated).
- Server room access controls (badge, biometrics, two-factor for high-classification).
- Cable management and tamper-evident infrastructure.
- Workstation physical security (anti-tamper, port restrictions).
- Removable media physical controls.
- Disposal of equipment.

## Where to look

- Data centre certification documents (Zone 3, Zone 4 for higher classifications).
- Building access logs and visitor registers.
- CCTV coverage maps.
- Asset register with location.
- Disposal records and certificates of destruction.

## Common evidence

- Data centre certification letter.
- Badge access logs for the server room over a sample period.
- Inspection records for tamper-evident seals.
- Disposal records with certificate of destruction for retired drives.

## Common pitfalls

- Cloud-hosted system documented without confirming the certified zone of the cloud
  region used.
- Server room shared with non-ICT functions.
- Removable media policy that exists but is not enforced.
- Disposal handled by a third party with no chain of custody.
