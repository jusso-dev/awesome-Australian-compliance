# Guidelines for Communications Infrastructure

This guideline covers cabling, conduits, communications cabinets and the physical
infrastructure that carries data, particularly where multiple classifications coexist.

## In plain English

Cables carry traffic of different sensitivities. Keep them separated, labelled and
documented. Don't let the patch panel become the path of least resistance.

## Why it exists

Cross-domain cable leakage is a real risk in mixed environments. The guideline pushes
agencies to plan cable infrastructure deliberately rather than letting it accumulate.

## Key topics

- Cable colour coding by classification.
- Cabinet labelling and access control.
- Separation between classified and unclassified infrastructure.
- Fibre vs. copper considerations at different classifications.
- Patch records and change control.

## Where to look

- Floor plans and cabling diagrams.
- Cabinet inventory with classification per port.
- Cabling install records.
- Photographic evidence of cable labelling and separation.

## Common evidence

- Up-to-date physical network diagrams.
- Cable register with classification per run.
- Patch panel audit records.

## Common pitfalls

- Cloud-centric system documented without considering the on-premises link to the cloud
  provider (Direct Connect, ExpressRoute, Interconnect).
- "Self-documenting" cabinet that hasn't been audited in five years.
- Cabling decisions made by facilities without security input.
