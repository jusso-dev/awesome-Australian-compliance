# Guidelines for Cyber Security Incidents

This guideline covers how an organisation prepares for, responds to and reports cyber
security incidents.

## In plain English

Have a plan. Practise it. Keep contact details current. Report incidents to the ACSC as
required, and to the OAIC if personal information is involved.

## Why it exists

Incident-handling capability is the difference between a contained event and a multi-day
public crisis. The guideline pushes agencies to write the plan, exercise it, and align
with ACSC's role as the national coordinator.

## Key topics

- Incident management policy and plan.
- Incident response training and exercises.
- Detection sources and triage procedure.
- Reporting to the ACSC (ReportCyber, telephone hotline) and to the OAIC where personal
  information is involved (NDB scheme).
- Post-incident review and lessons-learned.
- Evidence preservation and chain of custody.

## Where to look

- Incident response plan documents.
- Runbooks for common incident types (ransomware, account compromise, data exfiltration,
  insider, supply-chain).
- On-call rosters and contact registers.
- Tabletop exercise reports and remediation lists.
- SIEM detection content (Sigma rules, KQL queries, EQL rules) and tested response
  playbooks.

## Common evidence

- Signed incident response plan, dated within the agency's review cycle.
- Tabletop exercise minutes for the last 12 months.
- Sample of incident tickets with full triage, containment, eradication and recovery
  notes.
- ReportCyber submission record for a recent incident.
- NDB notification register where applicable.

## Common pitfalls

- Incident response plan written for compliance, never rehearsed.
- Contact details out of date.
- No documented decision-maker for "shutdown vs. monitor" calls.
- Evidence preservation not considered; logs and disk images destroyed during
  remediation.
- ReportCyber and OAIC reporting requirements forgotten.
