# Australian Privacy Principles to GDPR mapping (cell references only)

This table maps each Australian Privacy Principle (APP) to the closest GDPR article or
chapter. It exists for the common case of an Australian organisation explaining their
APP posture to a European counterparty, or vice versa.

This pack does not paraphrase the GDPR. The table is a cell-reference mapping only.
Refer to the live GDPR text on eur-lex.europa.eu and the European Data Protection
Board's guidelines for the actual wording.

## Mapping table

| APP | Subject | Closest GDPR article(s) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| APP 1 | Open and transparent management | Article 12 (transparent information), Articles 13 - 14 (information to be provided) |
| APP 2 | Anonymity and pseudonymity | Recital 26 (anonymous data outside scope), Article 4(5) (pseudonymisation definition) |
| APP 3 | Collection of solicited personal information | Article 5 (principles), Article 6 (lawful basis), Article 9 (special categories) |
| APP 4 | Unsolicited personal information | No exact analogue; Article 5(1)(c) data minimisation is the closest principle |
| APP 5 | Notification of collection | Articles 13 - 14 (information to be provided) |
| APP 6 | Use or disclosure | Article 5 (purpose limitation), Article 6 (lawful basis), Article 9 (special categories) |
| APP 7 | Direct marketing | Article 21 (right to object), Article 6(1)(f) (legitimate interests) |
| APP 8 | Cross-border disclosure | Chapter V (Articles 44 - 50, international transfers) |
| APP 9 | Government related identifiers | Article 87 (national identification numbers) |
| APP 10 | Quality of personal information | Article 5(1)(d) (accuracy) |
| APP 11 | Security of personal information | Article 5(1)(f) (integrity and confidentiality), Article 32 (security of processing), Article 25 (data protection by design and by default) |

## Differences worth noting

- **Lawful basis.** The Privacy Act's primary-purpose rule and GDPR's six lawful bases
  do not line up neatly. APP 6 has consent and specific exceptions; GDPR Article 6 has
  six bases including legitimate interests, which has no direct analogue in the Privacy
  Act.
- **Special categories / sensitive information.** Both regimes have a similar concept,
  but the lists differ in details.
- **Right to erasure (Article 17 GDPR).** No direct analogue in the Privacy Act (the
  closest is APP 11.2, which is a destruction obligation on the entity rather than a
  right exercisable by the individual, though OAIC guidance recognises individuals can
  request deletion in many contexts).
- **Data portability (Article 20 GDPR).** No direct analogue in the Privacy Act, though
  the Consumer Data Right covers similar territory for in-scope sectors.
- **Data Protection Officer.** GDPR Article 37 requires one in defined circumstances.
  The Privacy Act has no equivalent role, though most APP entities have a privacy
  officer in practice.
- **NDB notification thresholds.** Both regimes use risk-based thresholds but apply
  them differently. GDPR Articles 33 - 34 use risk to rights and freedoms and impose a
  72-hour clock to notify the supervisory authority. The Privacy Act uses serious-harm
  likelihood with a 30-day assessment window.
